## About This Repository

This repository documents my coursework, research, laboratory exercises,
and technical reflections from AI in Cybersecurity at Houston City College.

The course examined the intersection of artificial intelligence and
cybersecurity from two important perspectives:

1. How AI and machine learning can strengthen cybersecurity practices.
2. How AI systems introduce new vulnerabilities, attack surfaces,
   and security challenges.

My primary interests developed around AI application security,
large language model vulnerabilities, software supply chain security,
risk governance, and the responsible deployment of AI systems.

This repository serves as an academic record and professional portfolio
highlighting my understanding of AI security concepts, emerging threats,
and practical security frameworks.

**Status:** Completed

Explored vulnerabilities and security risks associated with
applications that integrate large language models.

This coursework introduced challenges that differ from traditional

- Model misuse and application-level vulnerabilities
- Risk mitigation and responsible implementation

## 5. Immersive Labs — Prompt Injection

**Status:** Completed

Completed hands-on exercises examining how prompt injection attempts
can influence the behavior of AI applications.

These exercises reinforced the importance of distinguishing between
trusted instructions and untrusted information processed by an AI system.

### Learning Focus

- Understanding prompt injection mechanisms
- Recognizing instruction-manipulation attempts
- Evaluating risks from untrusted content
- Understanding limitations of prompt-based defenses
- Identifying security considerations for LLM applications

**Platform:** Immersive Labs

### Key Takeaway

An AI application's ability to understand natural language also creates
a security challenge: untrusted content may contain instructions
that conflict with the application's intended behavior.

Protecting AI systems requires security controls beyond simply
instructing a model to ignore malicious input.

## 6. Research: LLMs as Cyber Weapons

**Status:** Completed

Explored the potential use of large language models in malicious
cybersecurity activities.

The research examined how generative AI can influence the scale,
accessibility, and sophistication of certain cyber threats.

### Topics Explored

- AI-assisted social engineering
- Automated generation of malicious content
- Dual-use AI capabilities
- Emerging cybersecurity risks
- Responsible development and deployment
- Organizational preparedness

### Key Takeaway

AI technologies can create significant benefits for cybersecurity
while simultaneously introducing capabilities that malicious
actors may attempt to exploit.

Understanding these dual-use implications is an important component
of AI security governance.

## 7. AI Governance & Policy Analysis

**Status:** Completed

Examined AI governance and policy considerations related to
cybersecurity, accountability, and organizational risk.

Course discussions and research included analysis of federal AI
policy and the responsibilities of organizations developing
or deploying AI systems.

### Topics Explored

- AI risk governance
- Security and accountability
- Federal AI policy
- Responsible AI implementation
- Organizational oversight
- Governance challenges created by rapidly evolving technology

### Key Takeaway

Technical security measures alone are insufficient to manage
the risks introduced by AI.

Effective governance requires clear organizational responsibilities,
risk evaluation processes, accountability mechanisms, and
ongoing oversight.

## Personal Reflection

One of my most important takeaways from AI in Cybersecurity
was recognizing that securing AI requires a broader perspective
than traditional cybersecurity practices alone.

AI systems introduce complex relationships between software,
training data, models, external services, users, and organizational
decision-making.

The course reinforced my interest in understanding these
interdependencies and evaluating how security failures can
emerge across an entire system.

I am particularly interested in AI governance and software
supply chain security because these areas connect technical
risk management with organizational accountability and
responsible technology deployment.

My long-term goal is to apply my AI education and professional
experience to work involving secure, reliable, and responsibly
managed AI systems.
